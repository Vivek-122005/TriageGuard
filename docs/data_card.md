# Data card: TriageGuard synthetic claim-document corpus (m2-v1)

> There are two corpora. This card describes the in-house one (`data/corpus/`). The Synthea + Faker corpus (`data/corpus_synthea/`, 1,096 patients, 5,105 documents) uses the same record format, layouts, noise and splits. Only its facts come from Synthea and its contact identifiers from Faker. See [realism_comparison.md](realism_comparison.md) for how the two differ and which is more realistic.

## Why synthetic

No public dataset has claim-style documents with PII, document types, fields and routes all labelled (M1 section 8.2). Generating our own gives exact labels, because every identifier and field is inserted by the generator at a known character offset, and clean licensing.

## How it is built

1. **Patients** (`patients.py`): name, date of birth, SSN, phone, email, address, member ID, MRN, account number, and a relative. SSNs use the 900-999 area range and phones use the 555 exchange, which are never issued, so no identifier can match a real person.
2. **Episodes**: one diagnosis (20 ICD-10 codes, 3 wordings each), 1-3 compatible procedures (26, grouped by clinical family), admission and discharge dates, a provider that fits the specialty, itemised charges, lab results, and medicines.
3. **Injected warning signs** (per episode): procedure that does not match the diagnosis family (7%), duplicated billing line (6%), impossible dates (5%: discharged before admitted, or service before birth). 22% of episodes have their total pushed into the $19k-$31k band around the high-value line so that routing is not a single threshold.
4. **Documents** (`layouts.py`): each episode yields a claim form and a bill, a discharge summary if there was a stay, and, with fixed probabilities, a lab report, a prescription and a pre-authorization letter. Each type has 4 layouts with different wording, ordering and formatting. Dates (6 formats), money (4), names (3), phones (4), SSN, member ID, MRN and account numbers (3 each) vary per document.
5. **Noise** (`noise.py`): `light` (40% of train/val/golden) and `heavy` (all of the hard set) apply typos, abbreviations (Pt, Dx, D/C, w/o...), odd line breaks and spacing, case changes, and dropped optional lines. Noise only touches text between labelled values, so spans stay exact.
6. **OCR-style text**: 40% of hard-set documents get one-for-one character confusions (O/0, l/1, S/5, e/c, ...) at a 3-9% rate. This keeps every span valid. Real Tesseract output is produced separately by `scripts/make_scans.py` (see Limitations).
7. **Routing labels** (`routing.py`): computed only from facts the document shows. Suspicious if it shows a warning sign. Otherwise high-value if the amount is at least $25,000, or at least $20,000 together with a 5+ day stay or several procedures or line items. Otherwise routine.
8. **Validation** (`validate.py`) runs before anything is written and fails generation on any span mismatch, unknown label, missing field key, real-range SSN, or split leak.

## Splits

| split | patients | documents | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 770 | 4,689 | training |
| val | 110 | 709 | tuning and threshold choice |
| test_golden | 110 | 682 | frozen final score, same distribution as train, never tuned on |
| test_hard | 110 | 722 | held-out layout (about half), heavy noise (all), OCR-style text (40%) |

* Splits are by patient: no patient appears in two splits.
* Layout 3 of every type is held out: it appears only in `test_hard`.
* There are no identical texts across splits.

## Record format (JSONL, one document per line)

```json
{
  "doc_id": "D000123", "patient_id": "P00042", "episode_id": "E000061",
  "split": "train", "doc_type": "hospital_bill", "layout": "hospital_bill/L1",
  "held_out_layout": false, "noise": "light", "ocr": false,
  "text": "...",
  "pii": [{"label": "SSN", "start": 81, "end": 92, "text": "912-45-0071"}],
  "fields": {"total_billed": 18450.0, "admission_date": "2026-03-02", "discharge_date": "2026-03-07",
             "line_items": [{"description": "Pharmacy", "amount": 412.5}]},
  "field_spans": [{"field": "line_items", "part": "amount", "item": 0, "start": 300, "end": 307, "text": "$412.50"}],
  "route": "high_value", "route_signals": [], "amount_high_value": true,
  "injected_signals": []
}
```

`injected_signals` records what the episode was given; `route_signals` records what this document actually shows. Models must be trained and scored on `route`, never on `injected_signals`.

## Intended use

Training and evaluating the TriageGuard stages: PII redaction (token NER), document-type classification, field extraction, routing and confidence calibration. It is not suitable for estimating real-world accuracy, clinical use, or fraud detection.

## Limitations and known biases

* **Easier than reality.** Templates, even with four layouts and noise, are far more regular than real paperwork. Golden-set scores will be optimistic, so the hard set must always be reported next to them (M1 section 7.3).
* **Routing labels are rules.** A model can learn them exactly. The value of the routing stage is the pipeline, the confidence score and the review loop. Real human decisions would replace these labels.
* **Clinical realism is shallow.** Diagnosis-procedure compatibility is by broad family, and lab values are drawn independently of the diagnosis.
* **Names** come from a fixed list of 70 first names and 70 surnames from several cultures. A model may overfit to them, so M3 should also test on unseen names.
* **OCR.** The built-in OCR noise is simulated. Real scan errors (merged words, lost lines, wrong reading order) are only covered once `scripts/make_scans.py` has been run with Tesseract installed.
* **Clinical realism is low compared with the Synthea corpus.** Diagnoses ignore age and sex (for example deliveries at age 70), there are no children, and lab values ignore the diagnosis. See [realism_comparison.md](realism_comparison.md).
